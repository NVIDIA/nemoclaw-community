Yes — (3) is the right call. The decoupling is the whole point of waves; if N+1 has to wait on N's tail we may as well not have batches.

On (a): same handler is fine, but I'd push them to the *back* of the backfill queue, not the front. Reasoning: the item already failed because the mutex was hot. If you front-load it, you're racing the same hot mutex before it's had time to cool. The age-based backfill items don't have that property — they're old precisely because nothing has tried them recently, so they're more likely to land clean. Letting `mutex_contention` items wait one cron cycle (~5min) is a feature, not a bug.

On (b): not over-engineering. Tomas paged me at 3am two weeks ago for the wiki-mutex thing on Beacon's side and the only signal he had was tail-latency on the wiki write endpoint, which is way downstream of the actual problem. A counter at the ingest layer means he can correlate cause and effect without grepping logs. Add it.

One more thought: the per-source wave-size config — where does it live? `src/atlas/config/sources.py`? Or are we going to use the source registry pattern Rohan added in Beacon last month? I'd lean toward the registry — keeps both projects converging — but it's your call since Atlas is moving faster than Beacon and the registry isn't quite stable yet.

Ship it.
