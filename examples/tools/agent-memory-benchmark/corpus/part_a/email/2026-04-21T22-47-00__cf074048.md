Read the revised doc and skimmed the MR. Mostly looks great. A few comments I left inline but the worth-discussing-here ones:

1. `WaveDispatcher.dispatch_next()` — you're acquiring the global mutex around the wave assembly, which means if the wiki writer is slow, the *dispatcher* blocks even though dispatch shouldn't need the mutex at all (it's just building the batch from in-memory state). I think you meant to acquire it only around the per-item commit step? Look at line ~140 in `wave_dispatcher.py`.

2. The mutex_contention counter — you put it in the wiki writer not the wave runner. Means it counts every contention event including ones from non-wave writers (e.g. the consolidate job). That's probably fine but the naming is misleading; consider `wiki_write_contention_total` and label by caller.

3. Test coverage for the cap-at-3 behavior. I see the unit test for the counter incrementing but not for the "moves to backfill at 3" transition. Easy to add — wave with a permanently-stuck mutex, assert the item ends up in backfill with the right marker.

4. Nit: the doc still says "the head of the wave is re-queued" in one place. Should be tail.

Not blocking on (1) but I want to understand it before I approve — could just be me misreading the contention boundary. Reply or grab me on chat tomorrow.

One side thought — once this lands, do we want to backport the per-source wave-size to Beacon? Rohan said the registry side stabilized last week, so we wouldn't be fighting a moving target.
